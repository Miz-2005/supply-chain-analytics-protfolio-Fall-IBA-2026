Illustrative supply chain map, calibrated to Engro Fertilizers Limited (EFERT)'s disclosed sector and scale — not actual company supplier, distribution, or process information.

---

### Product Focus & Calibrated Real Context

* **Product Selected:** Engro Prilled Urea (50 kg bag)
* **Broad Sector:** Agriculture / Chemical Fertilizers
* **Manufacturing Plants:** Main production hub in Daharki, Sindh (Base Plant and EnVen Plant); blended fertilizer facility at Port Qasim, Karachi.
* **Distribution Footprint:** Nationwide distribution across agricultural regions in Pakistan (Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan) via a dealer network.
* **Scale:** Large national player with an annual production capacity exceeding 2 million metric tons of Urea and annual revenue ~PKR 160–186 billion.

### 1. Upstream (Tier 2 and Tier 1 Suppliers)

* **Natural Gas / Feedstock**
* **Tier 1 Supplier Type:** Regional gas transmission and distribution utility (delivering high-pressure natural gas via pipeline directly to the manufacturing plant).
* **Tier 2 Source:** Domestic gas fields (e.g., Mari Gas field in northern Sindh/Balochistan border region) producing raw natural gas.


* **Polypropylene (PP) Woven Bags / Packaging Materials**
* **Tier 1 Supplier Type:** Domestic industrial woven sack manufacturers who convert polypropylene granules into 50 kg valve-type or open-mouth woven bags with PE liners.
* **Tier 2 Source:** Domestic or international petrochemical plants refining crude oil into polypropylene resin granules (imported or produced locally in Karachi).


* **Conditioning & Anti-Caking Chemicals**
* **Tier 1 Supplier Type:** Specialty chemical compounders supplying formaldehyde-based or organic anti-caking coatings to prevent urea granules/prills from clumping during storage and transport.
* **Tier 2 Source:** Chemical processing plants (domestic chemical clusters in Sindh/Punjab or specialty chemical exporters from Asia/Middle East).



---

### 2. Manufacturing (Internal Operations)

**Process Stages in Order:**

1. **Reforming & Gas Purification:** Natural gas is reacted with steam and air at high temperatures to produce synthesis gas (hydrogen and nitrogen), removing carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) in the process.
2. **Ammonia Synthesis:** Hydrogen and nitrogen are combined under high pressure and temperature over a catalyst to form liquid Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$).
3. **Urea Synthesis & Granulation/Prilling:** Ammonia and recovered $\text{CO}_2$ are reacted under pressure to form Ammonium Carbamate, which dehydrates into Urea melt. The molten urea is dropped down a prilling tower (or granulated) to cool and solidify into spherical prills.
4. **Coating & Conditioning:** Prills are sprayed with anti-caking compounds to ensure fluid handling during transit.
5. **Bagging & Palletizing:** Solid urea is automatically weighed, packed into 50 kg woven polypropylene bags, stitched, and loaded onto trucks or rail wagons.

* **Bottleneck / Most Time-Consuming Stage:** **Ammonia Synthesis and Primary Gas Reforming.** This thermochemical stage is extremely capital-intensive, energy-dense, and continuous. It requires steady gas pressure, precise temperature controls, and catalyst maintenance. Any interruption or pressure drop in natural gas supply halts the entire plant, making high-pressure gas availability the structural capacity bottleneck.

### 3. Downstream (Distribution to Customer)

* **Movement Path:** Factory (Daharki) $\rightarrow$ Regional Distribution Centers / Depots (located near major agricultural hubs) $\rightarrow$ District Wholesalers / Key Dealers $\rightarrow$ Village-Level Agri-Retailers $\rightarrow$ Farmer (End Customer).
* **Number of Tiers:** Roughly **3 to 4 tiers** (Depot $\rightarrow$ Wholesale Dealer $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Farmer) before reaching the end user.

---

### 4. Information & Financial Flow

* **Information Flow:** Demand projections flow backward from seasonal crop requirements through field sales agents, dealer purchase orders to regional depots, and ultimately feed into the Master Production Schedule (MPS) at the plant.
* **Financial Flow:** Dealers pay advance payments or operate on short credit terms (7–30 days depending on the cropping season), while factory payments flow upstream to gas utilities and packaging vendors via fixed contractual settlement cycles.

### 6. Lead Time Estimates

* **Link 1: Raw Material / Pipeline Gas to Factory (0.5 to 2 Days)**
* *Reasoning:* Natural gas arrives via continuous pipeline flow (real-time stream), but bulk items like packaging bags and coating chemicals take 1–3 days to deliver locally from domestic suppliers.


* **Link 2: Factory Production & Bagging (1 to 2 Days)**
* *Reasoning:* Continuous synthesis to prill formation occurs within hours; bagging, batch quality testing, and stage loading take around 24–48 hours.


* **Link 3: Factory to Regional Depot (2 to 5 Days)**
* *Reasoning:* Involves road transport via heavy multi-axle trucks (or rail cargo) traveling from northern Sindh to regional hubs across Punjab, Sindh, or KP, accounting for transit and depot offloading.


* **Link 4: Depot to Local Dealer / Retailer (1 to 3 Days)**
* *Reasoning:* Shorter secondary transport distances from regional warehouses directly to district markets using local flatbed trucks.


* **Link 5: Retailer to Farmer (0 to 2 Days)**
* *Reasoning:* Farmers purchase directly during planting season (immediate pickup) or order a few days ahead of application cycles.



---

### Assumptions

This map assumes a steady supply of natural gas at continuous pressure without severe load-shedding or pipeline disruptions, which is a major variable in Pakistan's energy sector. It assumes that all urea distribution occurs within the domestic market rather than being exported, as national policy prioritizes domestic agricultural needs. The distribution leg relies heavily on primary road freight logistics rather than rail transport, reflecting local infrastructure realities. Freight lead times do not account for extreme weather events, fuel shortages, or peak-season road congestion. A real internal supply chain map would capture granular multi-facility routing, SAP-integrated inventory safety stock triggers, multi-tier warehouse management, specific dealer credit limits, reverse logistics for damaged bags, and real-time gas pressure monitoring feeds.
