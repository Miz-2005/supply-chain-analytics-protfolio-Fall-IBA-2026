**Real context used to calibrate this map:** EFERT is a large national player in Pakistan's fertilizer/agri-input sector, headquartered in Karachi, majority-owned (~56%) by Engro Corporation. It operates two urea plants at **Daharki, Sindh** (the "Base" plant, ~975,000 MT/year, and "EnVen," ~1,300,000 MT/year — combined ~2.275 million MT/year urea capacity) plus a Zarkhez NPK blending plant at **Port Qasim, Karachi** (~100,000 MT/year). It holds roughly a third of Pakistan's fertilizer market and sells nationwide to farmers through a dealer network.

## 1. Upstream (Tier 2 and Tier 1 suppliers)

| Raw material category | Tier 1 supplier (converts input) | Tier 2 source (origin) |
|---|---|---|
| Natural gas (feedstock + fuel for ammonia synthesis) | Gas transmission/distribution utility supplying the Daharki plants under a gas supply agreement | Domestic gas fields in Sindh (piped via national gas transmission network) |
| Catalysts (for ammonia/urea synthesis reactors) | Specialty industrial-catalyst manufacturer/distributor | Imported — typically from Europe or China, since Pakistan has limited catalyst manufacturing |
| Bagging/packaging material (woven polypropylene bags, liners) | Domestic PP bag/woven-sack manufacturer (likely Karachi-based, near Port Qasim) | Polypropylene resin — largely imported petrochemical feedstock (Middle East/China), some domestic PP from local refineries |
| Plant machinery spares & maintenance consumables | Local industrial equipment/spare-parts distributor | Imported from Europe/Japan/China (specialized turbines, compressors, valves) |

## 2. Manufacturing (internal operations)

1. **Gas reception & purification** — incoming natural gas is treated to remove impurities before feeding the ammonia plant.
2. **Ammonia synthesis** — gas is reformed and reacted (Haber-Bosch process) to produce liquid ammonia, the core intermediate.
3. **Urea synthesis** — ammonia reacts with CO₂ (a byproduct of the reforming step) to form urea melt.
4. **Granulation/prilling** — urea melt is converted into solid granules/prills of consistent size.
5. **Bagging & warehousing** — granules are cooled, weighed, bagged (typically 50kg bags), and staged in on-site warehouses.

**Likely bottleneck:** the **ammonia synthesis stage**, because it's the most capital- and energy-intensive step, runs continuously at high pressure/temperature, and any gas-supply interruption or catalyst degradation there cascades through the rest of the process — unlike bagging, which can be buffered with inventory.

## 3. Downstream (distribution to customer)

Product moves from the Daharki plants to a network of **regional warehouses/distribution points**, then to **appointed dealers/distributors** across agricultural districts, who sell to **retail agri-input shops**, which sell to the **farmer** (end customer). Given the seasonal, geographically dispersed nature of farm demand, a unit typically passes through **3–4 tiers**: Factory → Regional warehouse → Dealer/Distributor → Retail shop → Farmer.

## 4. Information & Financial Flow

- **Demand information** likely flows backward as farmer purchase patterns and dealer stock-outs prompt distributor reorders, which are aggregated into periodic demand forecasts and replenishment orders placed with the factory/regional warehouses, especially ahead of the Rabi and Kharif sowing seasons.
- **Payment** likely flows via a mix of advance payments or short-term credit from dealers to the company (given the company's strong bargaining position and importance of urea supply), while dealers in turn extend limited credit or cash terms to retailers and farmers, particularly during peak sowing season.

## 6. Lead time estimates

| Link | Estimated lead time | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Gas supplier → Plant | Continuous (pipeline) | Gas is delivered via fixed pipeline infrastructure, not batch-shipped, so this is a continuous flow rather than a discrete lead time. |
| Catalyst/spares (imported) → Plant | 30–60 days | Ocean freight from Europe/China plus customs clearance at Karachi port. |
| Packaging material → Plant | 3–7 days | Domestic supplier, likely trucked from Karachi to Daharki. |
| Plant → Regional warehouse | 2–5 days | Long-haul trucking across Sindh/Punjab road network; distance and highway conditions vary by region. |
| Regional warehouse → Dealer/Distributor | 1–3 days | Shorter intra-province trucking runs, often faster given denser road access. |
| Dealer → Retail shop → Farmer | Same day–2 days | Local last-mile delivery, often farmer pickup, especially urgent during sowing windows. |
